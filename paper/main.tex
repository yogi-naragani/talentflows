\begin{abstract}
Drone autonomy stacks accumulate hand-written if/else trees as new
sensors and failure modes are added; the rule matrix grows
quadratically and becomes hard to test exhaustively. We replace this
tree with an on-device small language model (SLM) that runs at
0.5--2\,Hz, consumes a structured health observation, and emits a
structured action covering mission mode, waypoint nudges, MPC weight
overrides, and per-sensor trust. A separate safety supervisor clamps
every field of the SLM's action to a verified envelope, so the LLM
is contextual judgment but never the safety authority. We
empirically compare the SLM advisor against an effort-matched
rule-tree advisor consuming the identical observation, on
GPS-denied scenarios in Gazebo Harmonic with ORB-SLAM3
mono-inertial, a 1D rangefinder for monocular scale recovery, a
passive acoustic backup, and a perception-aware MPC. We report
mission completion, collision count, action quality vs
ground-truth, and clamp frequency. The system runs on a Raspberry
Pi\,5 with the AI HAT+ at 15--18\,W with the SLM (Gemma\,3\,1B
Q4\_K\_M) ticking in 0.3--0.6\,s.
\end{abstract}
