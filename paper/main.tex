\begin{abstract}
When monocular visual SLAM degrades on a GPS-denied micro-drone, the
vehicle loses both its pose estimate and its only proximity awareness
within milliseconds. We show that the same propeller noise that
complicates onboard drone audition can be turned into a passive
obstacle backup: a small microphone array listens for short-delay
reflections of the drone's own ego-noise and emits per-direction
proximity scores plus a worst-direction clearance estimate. The
signal is fused into a perception-aware MPC as a hard clearance
constraint that activates when SLAM tracking is lost or its
covariance grows beyond a threshold. We integrate the backup into a
ROS\,2 + Gazebo stack with ORB-SLAM3 mono-inertial, a 1D rangefinder
for monocular scale recovery, and an on-device small language model
acting as a low-rate mission supervisor. The contribution of this
paper is the empirical demonstration that the passive backup keeps
the drone safe through scripted SLAM-dropout scenarios where vision
alone would collide. We discuss the SNR floor, compare against the
active-ultrasound BatDeck baseline, and report power consumption on
the Raspberry Pi\,5 + Hailo AI HAT+ target.
\end{abstract}
