\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

GPS-denied flight is the common case for indoor inspection, subterranean
mapping, and contested outdoor operation. The classical recipe pairs
visual-inertial SLAM with a nonlinear controller, but two long-standing
weaknesses remain: monocular SLAM is scale-ambiguous, and purely
geometric stacks have no graceful response when tracking degrades.

We address both with minimal added hardware and on-device compute.
First, a single-beam 1D range sensor (a low-cost ToF or radar
altimeter) supplies a metric-scale anchor and a clearance floor.
Second, an on-device small language model -- Gemini Nano -- supervises
the stack at low rate, proposing mission-level adjustments (slow,
hover, return, or re-explore) from a compact JSON state summary.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
\begin{itemize}
\item A compact perception-aware MPC formulation that incorporates a
  beam-validity term alongside feature visibility.
\item A windowed scale-recovery scheme that uses the 1D range sensor
  against the locally planar ground assumption.
\item An on-device reasoning interface for Gemini Nano that is strictly
  advisory and bounded by safety constraints.
\item Simulation ablations isolating the contribution of each component.
\end{itemize}
