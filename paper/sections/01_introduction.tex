\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

GPS-denied flight is the common case for indoor inspection,
subterranean mapping, and contested outdoor operation. The classical
recipe pairs visual-inertial SLAM with a nonlinear controller, but
when SLAM degrades -- a textureless wall, sudden illumination change,
fast yaw -- the drone loses both its pose estimate and the
feature-based obstacle field that the controller relies on, often
within a single 30\,Hz frame interval.

Active ranging is one answer. BatDeck~\cite{batdeck2024} demonstrated
that a 30\,mW ultrasonic transducer is enough for nano-drone obstacle
avoidance. Active emission costs power, adds payload, and -- in
contested settings -- broadcasts the drone's location.

This paper asks: \emph{can the drone's own propeller noise be
exploited as a free, passive proximity signal?} Drone audition has
historically treated propeller ego-noise as a
nuisance~\cite{rotorconditioned2025} to be suppressed. We invert
that view. A small onboard microphone array listens specifically for
short-delay reflections of the rotor noise field, applies an
ego-reference whitening to the direct path, and cross-correlates the
residual to estimate per-direction proximity. The same hardware can
still serve other audition tasks; we add no emitter and no
single-purpose transducer.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
\begin{itemize}
\item A passive acoustic proximity pipeline using onboard microphones
  and propeller ego-noise as the illumination source, with
  per-direction proximity scores and a worst-direction clearance
  estimate.
\item Integration into a perception-aware MPC as a hard clearance
  constraint that activates only when visual SLAM degrades, keeping
  the nominal flight envelope unchanged.
\item Empirical evaluation on scripted SLAM-dropout scenarios in
  Gazebo Harmonic, with measured collision-avoidance against an
  ablation that disables the backup.
\item A frank comparison against the active-ultrasound BatDeck
  baseline along power, payload, acoustic signature, and multi-role
  use.
\item Open-source ROS\,2 release with a Raspberry Pi\,5 + Hailo
  AI HAT+ deployment target and measured power budget.
\end{itemize}

The other components of the stack -- ORB-SLAM3 mono-inertial, the
1D-rangefinder scale recovery, the perception-aware MPC, and the
on-device small language model that acts as a slow mission
supervisor -- are not the focus of this paper. They form the host
system in which the acoustic backup is evaluated.
