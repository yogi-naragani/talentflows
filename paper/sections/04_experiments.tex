\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:experiments}

\subsection{Setup}
We evaluate in two complementary harnesses (Sec.\,\ref{sec:impl}).
The \emph{Gazebo Harmonic} build (\texttt{ros2 launch
gps\_denied\_drone sim.launch.py}) bridges synchronized 30\,Hz
monocular images, 50\,Hz 1D range, 4-channel 48\,kHz audio, and
200\,Hz IMU through \texttt{ros\_gz\_bridge}, and runs the full
nonlinear MPC. The \emph{Python sim}
(\texttt{python -m experiments.sim.cli}) drives the same library
code -- Monitor, both advisors, SafetySupervisor, LQR controller --
against synthetic dynamics and sensors so an entire ablation grid
runs in seconds; this is what we use for the per-cell statistics
reported below.

Scenarios (declared in \texttt{experiments/sim/world.py} and
\texttt{degradation.py}):
\texttt{corridor\_white\_wall} (textureless wall mid-corridor),
\texttt{texture\_mask\_burst} (2\,s synthetic mask),
\texttt{illumination\_drop} (sudden lighting change),
\texttt{dual\_failure\_slam\_and\_range} (concurrent SLAM dropout
and range invalid). Each cell uses 25 trials over 5 fixed seeds.

\subsection{Hardware target}
Onboard compute is a Raspberry Pi 5 (8\,GB) with the Raspberry Pi
AI HAT+ (Hailo-8). Pi\,5 idle power is $\sim$3\,W; sustained four-core
load is 9--12\,W; the Hailo-8 contributes $\sim$5\,W typical and up to
8\,W peak under inference. Combined drone-stack budget is
\textbf{15--18\,W}, which is $\sim$10\% of the motor power for a
500\,g class quadrotor in hover. CPU shares are approximately
ORB-SLAM3 1.5 cores, acoustic processing 0.5 core, MPC 0.3 core,
DDS 0.2 core, and SLM advisory $\sim$1 core in burst.

Gemini Nano runs only on Pixel/Tensor hardware via Android AICore. On
Pi\,5 we deploy a Gemini Nano-class SLM (Gemma\,3\,1B, Q4\_K\_M, via
\texttt{llama.cpp}) in the same advisory role. We report wall-clock
SLM tick latency under contention with SLAM and MPC running. The full
breakdown is in \texttt{docs/hardware\_target.md}.

\subsection{Ablations}
The single comparison this paper isolates is
\textit{advisor=slm} vs \textit{advisor=rule\_tree}, declared in
\texttt{experiments/configs/paper\_slm\_vs\_rules.yaml}. Both
advisors consume the identical observation produced by the
\texttt{HealthMonitor} and emit the identical
\texttt{ReasoningAction} schema; they differ only in how they map
observations to actions. The SafetySupervisor, MPC, perception
stack, 1D rangefinder, acoustic backup, and dynamics are held
fixed across both arms (the rule-tree advisor itself lives at
\texttt{gps\_denied\_drone/reasoning/rule\_tree.py} -- not a
strawman; its thresholds were derived from the same decision
guidance written into the SLM's system prompt).

\subsection{Metrics}
Absolute trajectory error (ATE-RMSE), mission completion rate,
collision count, minimum clearance, MPC solve time, advisor tick
latency, and SafetySupervisor clamp frequency. The clamp frequency
is the central ``did the advisor stay in the envelope on its own?''
diagnostic.

\subsection{Results}

\paragraph{Host-system safety baseline
(Table~\ref{tab:acoustic}, Fig.~\ref{fig:acoustic-traj}).}
Before isolating the advisor's contribution we verify that the
shared host system (Monitor + acoustic backup + LQR with
controller-rate clearance enforcement + SafetySupervisor) is
itself working as intended.
Under the central acoustic on/off comparison
(\texttt{paper-acoustic-backup-VgEQR}, 10 seeds per cell, 30\,s
trials, point-mass dynamics with controller-rate clearance
enforcement, acoustic proximity reads corrupted by additive
Gaussian noise to model the $-20$\,dB SNR floor of passive
ego-noise reflectometry), enabling the acoustic backup eliminated
collisions in all $40$ trials (100\% vs.\ 100\%) and raised
minimum clearance from $0.154 \pm 0.008$\,m to $0.641 \pm
0.037$\,m. A paired Wilcoxon signed-rank test on the matched
acoustic-on / acoustic-off trials gives $p < 10^{-6}$ for
collisions and $p < 10^{-6}$ for minimum clearance ($n = 40$
pairs, see \texttt{experiments/sim/paired\_stats.py}). The
qualitative behavior is visible in
Fig.~\ref{fig:acoustic-traj}: the acoustic-off trajectories
converge into the wall and terminate at the collision marker,
while the acoustic-on trajectories stop cleanly $\sim$0.6\,m
before it.

Mission completion was $0$ in every cell because the Python
harness has no path planner: the wall blocks the straight-line
path to the third waypoint, so the system can only choose between
colliding (acoustic off) and stopping safely short (acoustic on).
We therefore frame the claim as \emph{safety preservation under
SLAM degradation, not mission completion}.

The four scenarios produced identical statistics because the
acoustic clearance constraint in the LQR controller is enforced
at controller rate regardless of SLAM state -- it is the floor
that all four scenarios share. Per-scenario differences will
appear once the Gazebo build is run end to end (where the SLAM
backend, the MPC's perception cost, and the advisor's mode
selection all interact more richly with scenario-specific
degradation), and once a local planner is added so completion
can succeed on the right side of the comparison.

\paragraph{Advisor comparison (Table~\ref{tab:advisor}).}
The central result of this paper. The rule-tree advisor and the
SLM advisor consume the identical observation stream and emit the
identical action schema, differing only in policy. We evaluate on
four in-distribution scenarios (the rule-tree thresholds were
tuned for these by hand) and four out-of-distribution scenarios
(novel compound failures the rule tree was not tuned for); see
\texttt{experiments/configs/paper\_slm\_vs\_rules.yaml}. We report
mission completion, safety-clamp frequency, action quality versus
an offline oracle, advisor tick latency on the Pi\,5 + AI HAT
target, and the generalization gap (ID minus OOD) for each
advisor.

\textit{The numbers below are pending the on-device SLM run.} The
rule-tree column is fully populated by the simulator
(\texttt{python -m experiments.sim.cli --advisors rule\_tree}).
The SLM column requires \texttt{llama\_cpp\_python} and a Gemini
Nano-class GGUF (default: Gemma\,3\,1B Q4\_K\_M) on the deployment
target; the harness (\texttt{create\_backend} factory in
\texttt{gps\_denied\_drone/reasoning/llama\_cpp\_backend.py}) is in
place but is not exercised in CI. The camera-ready will replace
the placeholder cells with measured values from the Pi\,5 + AI
HAT+ rig.
