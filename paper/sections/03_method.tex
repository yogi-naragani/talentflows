\section{Method}
\label{sec:method}

\subsection{System overview}
Four modules run concurrently: visual SLAM (front + back end),
1D-rangefinder filter, state estimator, and an MPC controller.
A fifth module -- the Gemini Nano reasoner -- runs at low rate and
publishes advisory mission updates.

\subsection{Metric scale from a 1D beam}
Let $r_k$ be the measured range and $\hat r_k$ the SLAM-predicted range
to the local ground plane (intersected with the beam). Over a sliding
window of $W$ samples we estimate the scale
\[
  s = \mathrm{mean}\bigl\{\, r_k / \hat r_k \mid k \in \mathcal{I} \,\bigr\},
\]
where $\mathcal{I}$ is the inlier set under a relative-tolerance test
against the median. The estimated $s$ is multiplied into the SLAM map
and pose stream once it stabilizes.

\subsection{Perception-aware MPC}
The cost over the horizon $N$ is
\[
  J = \sum_{k=0}^{N-1}
    \| x_k - x_k^{\text{ref}} \|_Q^2
  + \| u_k \|_R^2
  + w_p \, \phi(x_k, \mathcal{M}, \mathbf{b}),
\]
where $\phi$ penalizes both predicted feature loss and beam-invalid
geometry (e.g.\ pitching the body so the beam leaves the ground). $Q$,
$R$, $w_p$ are listed in Table~\ref{tab:mpc-params}.

\subsection{Acoustic backup proximity}
A passive microphone array on the airframe captures the drone's
ego-noise (propeller wash) reflected from nearby surfaces. After
band-pass filtering to the propeller harmonic band and ego-reference
whitening, cross-correlation across mic pairs yields per-direction
proximity scores $\rho_d \in [0,1]$ for $d \in \{\text{front, back,
left, right, up, down}\}$, and where consistent across pairs, a
time-of-flight clearance estimate $c_d$.

This signal is a *backup*: while SLAM tracking is healthy it is
ignored. When SLAM degrades or its estimated covariance grows beyond a
threshold, the MPC consumes $c_d$ as a hard lower bound on body-axis
clearance, and the reasoner sees $\rho_d$ in the state summary so it
can prefer retreat directions away from the most-occluded axis. This
keeps the drone safe through brief perception outages without
requiring any extra ranging emitter.

\subsection{On-device reasoning loop}
The reasoner replaces what would otherwise be a hand-written rule tree
over sensor states. We split it into three layers (Fig.\,\ref{fig:reasoning},
described in detail in \texttt{docs/llm\_decision\_design.md}):

\begin{enumerate}
\item A \textbf{Monitor} aggregates raw signals into a structured
  observation $\mathbf{o}_t$: pose, velocity, SLAM tracking flag and
  inlier \emph{trend}, range valid-ratio, per-direction acoustic
  proximity, battery, IMU vibration, motor saturation, and mission
  state. The Monitor performs no policy logic.
\item An \textbf{SLM advisor} (Gemini Nano on Pixel; a Gemini
  Nano-class SLM such as Gemma\,3\,1B via \texttt{llama.cpp} on
  Pi\,5+AI HAT) consumes $\mathbf{o}_t$ and emits a structured
  action $a_t = \{\text{mode}, \Delta\text{wp}, v_{\max},
  \text{yaw}, \text{mpc\_weights}, \text{sensor\_trust},
  \text{replan}, \text{rationale}, \text{confidence}\}$. The action
  schema lets the SLM retune MPC weights and per-modality fusion
  trusts, not just request a mode change.
\item A \textbf{SafetySupervisor} clamps every field of $a_t$ to a
  verified envelope (mode whitelist, speed cap, waypoint-delta norm,
  altitude floor, geofence, weight and trust ranges). Battery
  thresholds override the SLM (forced \emph{return} or \emph{land}).
  The supervisor emits a clamp report so we can audit overrides
  offline.
\end{enumerate}

The SLM is advisory and runs at $0.5$--$2$\,Hz; the SafetySupervisor
and the MPC's own constraints are the safety authority. This split
keeps the hard real-time path classical and verifiable while letting
the SLM collapse a high-dimensional state matrix into a contextual
decision without us writing the conjunction by hand.
